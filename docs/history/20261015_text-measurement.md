# Text measurement model, punctuation compression, and line-break regression checks

**Date**: 2026-10-15 (empty-bullet rule added 2026-10-20)
**Status**: Accepted and implemented.
**Canonical code**: `src/pptx/text.rs` (measurement and rounding), `src/lib.rs` (`punctuation_compression` option, manifest field, warning), `tests/libreoffice.rs` (ignored comparison suites `automatic_punctuation_compression_matches_both_modes`, `boundary_widths_match_both_modes`, `punctuation_positions_match_both_modes`), `tests/visual/compare_boundary_widths.py`, `tests/visual/compare_punctuation_positions.py`, `tests/fixtures/metrics.ttf` (generated by `tests/fixtures/make_metrics_font.py`).

## Decision

- Widths use nominal advances fetched in font units and scaled in double precision. For Impress-like measurement the font height is rounded to 1/100 mm and then to integer coordinates on a 600 DPI reference device. The SVG glyph size is unchanged. Vertical placement prefers Windows OS/2 cell metrics, falling back to typographic/hhea metrics.
- Explicit character spacing `spc` (1/100 pt) applies only between characters of the same style span, never at line ends or style boundaries; excessively negative spacing warns and clamps the advance to 0.
- `--punctuation-compression` is an experimental rendering policy, default off, stored in the manifest, and emits an approximation warning when enabled. It compresses horizontal wrapped text with zero or unspecified `spc` only as far as needed, up to half-width on full-width punctuation and brackets (space before opening brackets, after closing brackets and punctuation). It never compresses kana, middle dots, `!?`, or `wrap="none"` text; nonzero explicit spacing wins and warns.
- For zero-spacing lines, box widths are rounded to 1/100 mm and cumulative candidate widths and per-character compression allowances are truncated in those units. The retention decision (a wrapped line keeping maximum compression when it regains its natural width) allows a one-unit boundary; that allowance applies to retention only, never to line-break or image thresholds.
- A paragraph draws a bullet marker only when it has a text run (`a:r` or `a:fld`), not when it has a non-whitespace glyph. Empty-line height and auto-numbering counters are unchanged.
- Regression checks go below whole-page metrics: per-line ink shares (difference at most 0.02) and normalized horizontal ink distributions (transport and edge offsets at most 2 px at 1200 px width). They detect a one-character move with an unchanged line count, and are not OCR.

## Why

- Whole-page MAE and changed-pixel ratios passed while line breaks were wrong; the 63-page boundary experiment had 27 mismatches under passing page metrics. Line-level and ink-distribution checks exist because the global thresholds hid the defects, and the thresholds themselves were not relaxed.
- The rounding model reproduces observed Impress behaviour (reference device, height conversion, cumulative width truncation, position restoration were consulted in LibreOffice sources and not ported) and is an approximation of one renderer, not a universal OOXML rule. Truncating each character separately would accumulate error; truncating the cumulative width fixed the remaining 12 pt boundaries without moving individual advances.
- Punctuation compression is not detectable from the PPTX, and PowerPoint's default was not established; LibreOffice's Asian Layout setting changed tight-line wrapping in experiments. Making it an explicit, off-by-default, warned option keeps default output honest about what was verified.
- The empty-bullet rule follows a direct PowerPoint observation (issue #53): a paragraph holding one space shows a bullet, a paragraph with no runs shows none. LibreOffice agreed on the fixture. Break-only paragraphs and other whitespace were not independently checked.
- Known remaining difference: nested brackets `「「あ」」い」` still differ from LibreOffice and are kept as a separate failing fixture (`examples/punctuation-position-nested.pptx`) rather than folded into passing cases.
