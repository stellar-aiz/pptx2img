# License rhodopsin under MIT

**Date**: 2026-10-30
**Status**: Decided; applied to both crates in the same change.

## Decision

- Both crates (`rhodopsin`, `rhodopsin-xlsx`) are licensed MIT. The root `LICENSE` and `crates/xlsx/LICENSE` carry the same text with the copyright notice "Stellar AIz and the rhodopsin contributors"; both `Cargo.toml` files declare `license = "MIT"`.
- `rhodopsin-xlsx` was already MIT when it was imported from xlsx2pdf (`2280105`); the root crate had no licence until now.
- `crates/xlsx/tools/dependency-artifacts.py` exempts workspace members from its permissive allow list: they are the licensed product, not third-party inventory. Both tools run from the repository root so the SBOM is rooted at the `rhodopsin` binary and the bundle carries the root `LICENSE`.
- The third-party attribution obligations (BSD-3-Clause for tiny-skia, Unicode-3.0 for unicode-ident, and the permissive rest) are met by the NOTICE file that the dependency tooling generates (#113).

## Why

- The stated requirement was asymmetric: casual use inside Stellar AIz (bundling in internal tooling that is also delivered to clients) and strict terms for everyone else. On examination the strict half had no concrete target: the foreseeable third parties use the binary unmodified, which even AGPL permits without obligation, and there is no plan to sell commercial licences or to stop a modified product.
- AGPL-3.0-only with dual licensing was drafted and rejected: its strictness would bind only parties who modify and redistribute or serve the code, while its costs were certain (AGPL rights passed to clients through the bundled copy and its SBOM, a commercial-licence promise to administer, a contributor licence agreement, and lower adoption and fewer bug reports from corporate users). It also contradicted the public positioning of the project as an Office-independent renderer for anyone.
- MIT matches the licence the xlsx crate already carried, needs no CLA, adds nothing to downstream SBOM notices beyond attribution, and is compatible with all 114 crates in the resolved dependency tree (MIT, Apache-2.0, BSD, Zlib, BSL-1.0, Unicode-3.0). Apache-2.0 was considered equivalent for this purpose; MIT was kept for consistency with the xlsx crate.
- A stricter licence can still be applied to future versions if a concrete need appears; versions already published stay MIT.

Canonical files: `LICENSE`, `crates/xlsx/LICENSE`, `Cargo.toml` and `crates/xlsx/Cargo.toml` (`license`), `README.md` (License section).
